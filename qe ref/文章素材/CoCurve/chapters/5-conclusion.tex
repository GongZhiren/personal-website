\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Structured LLM pruning has been operating at the wrong granularity: it ranks units as independent
nodes, while Transformer computation couples them through a shared residual stream. \methodfull
replaces that ranking with global co-pruning-risk minimisation, from a single Fisher matrix that is
node saliency and cross-module co-pruning curvature at once, recovered from single-unit ablations and
solved in one shot under a shared budget. How much to trust the off-diagonal is read off a finite path of solutions by a held-out, label-free
criterion, using the same rule at every compression ratio.
Across five models from $3$B to $24$B and three compression ratios, \method leads on
$38$ of $45$ perplexity cells and $32$ of $60$ capability-group cells against the
per-column best of every baseline, and on $666$ of $721$ cells against those baselines one at
a time.
The margin is largest where the field breaks: on Mistral-Small-24B at $\rho{=}40\%$ its perplexity is
$277.1$ against the best baseline's $3299$.
Our ablations attribute the gain to the off-diagonal rather than to node saliency, and it survives a
control that fixes the attention--FFN split at the diagonal selector's own allocation. The term acts
through a population we can name and delete on demand, and it carries positional structure the
diagonal does not: coupling falls with depth on all ten models, while saliency does not
even keep its sign. Under one shared budget the objective then recovers, without being told to, an
allocation that spends the first half of the depth on FFN width alone: of the twenty-five solutions
across five models and five ratios, seventeen remove attention at all, and thirteen of those
take none of it from the first half.

None of this is tied to one modality. The same three objects --- a KL risk, a Gram matrix of
single-unit ablations, a budgeted greedy --- transfer to vision--language models unchanged, where they
also measure something a single stack cannot: the curvature coupling a language unit to a vision unit,
which runs $\vlmCrossLo$--$\vlmCrossHi$ of the coupling inside a tower and is why one budget shared
across both towers is a measurement rather than a preference.

\textbf{Limitations and outlook.} Estimating the edge matrix costs hours rather than minutes, but only
once per model. It is independent of the ratio and of $\lambda$, so one estimate serves the whole
path. Turning a mask into a smaller model needs a slicing path per operator family. What a removable unit
\emph{is} is the open question: under mixture-of-experts routing the units are already sparsely
activated, and in stacks that interleave attention with linear-recurrent layers some layers have no
heads to remove. The risk of \cref{eq:risk} is defined in both cases; the inventory it is minimised
over is not, and extending it there is the natural next step. The inventory changes; the object does not. Wherever units share a
residual stream, the damage from removing a set is not the sum of the damages, and the term that says so
is measurable without a single gradient: \emph{prune the edges, not just the nodes}.
