\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Training-free structured pruning of LLMs.} The dominant paradigm scores each removable unit
independently and deletes the lowest-ranked ones, using first/second-order Taylor
importance~\citep{ma2023llmpruner}, activation statistics~\citep{an2024flap,sun2024wanda}, rotate-and-slice
projections~\citep{ashkboos2024slicegpt}, per-layer OBS~\citep{ling2024slimgpt,frantar2023sparsegpt,frantar2022obc},
or least-squares output reconstruction~\citep{tang2025slimllm}. A parallel line prunes whole
layers~\citep{men2024shortgpt,kim2024shortened,yang2024laco} or searches a global layer-wise
budget~\citep{li2025tyr,jansen2026blockcbo}. All share a node-first assumption. Set-level pruning damage is treated as additive, and even when
several module types are scored they are merged into one independent ranking. These are our baselines
and concurrent comparisons. \method departs by making the cross-unit interaction a first-class, derived
quantity. \Cref{tab:positioning} positions it against
interaction-aware and modern structured pruners.

\textbf{Beyond the diagonal: interaction-aware pruning.} \method descends from classical second-order saliency~\citep{lecun1989obd,hassibi1993obs} and
Fisher/Taylor importance for modern networks~\citep{theis2018faster,molchanov2019importance}. Diagonal
\method \emph{is} an OBD-style structured saliency (\cref{eq:decomp}). Curvature beyond the diagonal is
not new either. What \method contributes is a specific object, and it is worth naming its properties
because they are what the rest of this section is measured against: an attention$\leftrightarrow$FFN,
cross-layer Fisher block at \emph{unit-pair} granularity, recovered from $M$ single-unit ablations with
no gradients and no pairwise sweep, and used to decide \emph{which individual units} go under a
training-free selection-only solve.

Each of the four properties is where a near neighbour stops. LLM-Surgeon's KFAC curvature is
Kronecker-factored within a single weight matrix and explicitly disregards cross-layer
interactions~\citep{vanderouderaa2024llmsurgeon}: no cross-module block. SlimGPT models coupling inside
a layer and compensates as it prunes~\citep{ling2024slimgpt}, and ZipLM couples layers only through a
runtime-aware sparsity profile rather than an explicit curvature term~\citep{kurtic2023ziplm}: no
cross-layer curvature, and neither is selection-only. SAViT comes closest in spirit --- an explicit
cross-component second-order block for vision Transformers --- but collapses each block to its scalar
mean~\citep{zheng2022savit}, so its interaction sets \emph{how much} of each component type to remove
while the choice among individual units stays independent: no unit-pair granularity. Two works come at the object we build from
opposite sides, and together they say why it has stayed out of reach. \citet{kwon2022fastpruning} write
the pairwise Fisher between \emph{unit} mask variables and note in as many words that the interaction
between two heads that cover for one another lives in its non-diagonal elements --- then approximate that
matrix block-diagonally, one block per attention or FFN layer, with no interaction between mask variables
in different layers. \citet{yang2023nvit} write the same object for vision Transformers, observe that a
model has tens of thousands of structural groups, declare computing it directly infeasible, and retreat
to a per-unit norm. The block \method estimates is exactly what both of them excluded, and the Gram
construction of \cref{sec:method-estimate} is why we can afford it: $M$ forward ablations, no gradients, no
pairwise sweep. \Cref{app:related-ext} does the same accounting for the remaining neighbours ---
SOSP~\citep{nonnenmacher2022sosp}, the Combinatorial Brain Surgeon~\citep{yu2022cbs}, whose
weight-level \emph{cancellation} argument is the ancestor of ours, and Optimal Brain
Connection~\citep{obc2025}, which assumes the block-diagonal form we discard.

\textbf{Recovering what compression costs.} Restoring capability after pruning belongs to pipelines
that retrain, such as the distillation and continued pretraining of
Minitron~\citep{muralidharan2024minitron} or evolutionary search~\citep{tang2025darwinlm}. That is a
different regime from the selection-only one studied here, where the question is which units to remove
before any weight moves~\citep{jaiswal2024kick}. \Cref{sec:exp-recovery} reports what a light recovery
stage adds when it is enabled for every method at once.

\textbf{Compressing a vision--language model.} The dominant axis there is the visual token sequence
rather than the weights: FastV~\citep{chen2024fastv}, SparseVLM~\citep{zhang2025sparsevlm} and
MADTP~\citep{cao2024madtp} shorten the sequence a decoder attends to and leave every parameter in
place. That is orthogonal to removing structure --- one shortens the input, the other shrinks the
network --- and the two compose. Where vision--language work does remove weights it trains:
UPop~\citep{shi2023upop} searches subnets and retrains progressively, and
EfficientLLaVA~\citep{liang2025efficientllava} searches a per-component pruning policy while
optimising the vision projector. \Cref{sec:exp-vlm} is the training-free structural setting in that
picture, and the property that makes it a test of this paper rather than a second paper is that one
$\Hmat$ spans both towers, so the budget split between them is an output of the objective.
