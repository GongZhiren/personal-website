\begin{abstract}
Structured pruning removes whole attention heads and feed-forward channel groups, but training-free
methods rank those units independently, as if the damage from removing a set were the sum of the damage
from removing each one. Transformer sublayers are coupled through a shared residual stream, so two units
that look individually weak can be jointly indispensable, and independent scoring deletes them together.
We introduce \method (Cross-Module Co-Pruning Curvature), which prunes attention and FFN units under one
shared budget. Expanding the token-level KL between the frozen model and its masked copy to second order
yields a single Fisher matrix whose diagonal is classical node saliency and whose off-diagonal is the
\emph{co-pruning curvature} between units. We estimate that matrix as a Gram product of single-unit
ablation features, so it follows from $M$ ablations rather than $O(M^2)$ pairwise sweeps --- no gradients, no
weight update --- and pruning becomes one budgeted quadratic program solved in a single shot. How far to
trust the off-diagonal is measured rather than tuned: $\lambda$ indexes a finite family of prunings
whose breakpoints we enumerate exactly, and we keep the member with the lowest held-out calibration
risk, using no labels and no benchmarks.
Across five models from $3$B to $24$B at three ratios, \method leads on $38$ of $45$
perplexity cells against a row holding, in each cell, whichever of seven training-free baselines wins
it, and on $666$ of $721$ cells against those baselines one at a time; the strongest of them
backpropagates through the whole model and so needs $94$\,GB to select at $24$B, more than an $80$\,GB
card has. Read along the other axis, at equal perplexity \method removes $2.2$ to $6.6$ more
points of the network than the strongest baseline at $\rho{=}30$--$40\%$. The off-diagonal also acts on
a population we can name and delete on demand: forcing out the low-saliency, high-connectivity
\emph{bridge} units against a cost- and type-matched control costs nineteen of twenty capability-group
cells across five models, while on three of the five perplexity moves the \emph{other} way.
Nothing in the construction is specific to a decoder-only stack: it transfers unchanged to seven
vision--language models under one budget shared across both towers, leading every training-free
baseline on five of them at $\rho{=}20\%$ with no baseline ahead of it by as much as a point on any.
\method reframes structured LLM pruning: \emph{prune the edges, not just the nodes.}
\end{abstract}
