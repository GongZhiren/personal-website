\section{The One-Shot Collapse}
\label{sec:collapse}

\subsection{Calibration-Only Structured Pruning}
We prune a frozen pretrained model $p_0$ (the \emph{dense teacher}) using only a small unlabeled
calibration set $\mathcal{C}$ and no gradient updates. The model is partitioned into $M$ structured
\emph{units} (attention heads and FFN channel groups); a pruning state is a mask
$s\in\{0,1\}^M$ ($s_u{=}1$ means unit $u$ is removed); each unit has a deployment cost $c_u$ (its
parameters/FLOPs). 
Write $C = \sum_u c_u$, $c_{\max} = \max_u c_u$, and $B = \rho^\star C$ for the target removal cost ($\rho^\star$ the target sparsity, written $\rho$ when varied). Because units are indivisible, the returned mask satisfies $B \le c^\top s < B + c_{\max}$, overshooting the target by less than one unit.
Because no labels are available, damage is measured by the model's drift from its own
dense behavior, i.e.\ the calibration KL to the teacher,
\begin{equation}
\Risk(s) \;=\; \E_{x\sim\mathcal{C}}\,\KL\!\big(p_0(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,p_s(\cdot\mid x)\big),
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
a self-distillation objective~\citep{hinton2015distillation} that needs only forward passes; we seek the
least-damaging mask in this target-cost shell. This is the setting of
LLM-Pruner~\citep{ma2023llmpruner}, FLAP~\citep{an2024flap}, Wanda~\citep{sun2024wanda}, and
SliceGPT~\citep{ashkboos2024slicegpt} once their recovery stages are disabled.

\subsection{A Unified Second-Order View}
The calibration-only pruners above look different but share one object: a single quadratic model of the
pruning loss, built once at the dense model. Making this explicit exposes the structural weakness they
inherit.
Let $z_s$ be the logits of model $p_s$ and $\Fisher^0=\mathrm{diag}(p_0)-p_0p_0^\top$ the dense
Fisher (the Hessian of \eqref{eq:risk} in logit space at $p_0$). Pruning a small set of units perturbs
the logits by $\dz=z_0-z_s$, and a second-order expansion of \eqref{eq:risk} about the dense model
gives the familiar pruning quadratic~\citep{lecun1989obd,hassibi1993obs,singh2020woodfisher}
\begin{equation}
\Risk(s)\;\approx\;\tfrac12\,s^\top \Hmat\, s,\qquad
\Hmat_{uv}=\E_{x}\big[\dz_u^\top \Fisher^0 \dz_v\big],
\label{eq:oneshot}
\end{equation}
where $\dz_u$ is the marginal logit change of removing unit $u$ from the dense model. The diagonal
$\Hmat_{uu}$ is the classical single-unit saliency; the off-diagonal $\Hmat_{uv}$ is the curvature of
\emph{co-removing} $u$ and $v$. First-order Taylor saliency, magnitude, and activation statistics are
recovered as diagonal or rank-one approximations of \eqref{eq:oneshot}.
Throughout, $\norm{x}_{A}\!:=\!\sqrt{x^\top A x}$ is the seminorm induced by a positive-semidefinite matrix $A$, and the \emph{Fisher length} of a path is its arc length in this metric.
% \textbf{The defining property of one-shot pruning is that \eqref{eq:oneshot} is built once, at the dense model, and then used to select the entire target-cost mask.}
At the dense anchor the first-order term
$\nabla\Risk(0)$ vanishes \emph{by construction} and pruning reduces to the single quadratic
\eqref{eq:oneshot}. \method exploits this structure by repeatedly refreshing unit perturbations at the
current pruned model while retaining the dense-anchor Fisher metric
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).

\subsection{The Collapse Is Super-Exponential}
\label{sec:superexp}
A quadratic is faithful only within a trust region of its expansion point, whose radius does not grow
with the budget. As $\rho$ increases, the optimal mask $s^\star(\rho)$ moves a Fisher-distance
$\norm{s^\star}_{\Hmat}$ that scales with $\rho$, so the neglected third- and higher-order terms grow as
the cube of that distance, the analytic origin of the accelerating inflation factors below.
On LLaMA-2-7B (Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}) one-shot second-order WikiText perplexity rises
$6.4\!\to\!9.3\!\to\!19.9\!\to\!92.6\!\to\!1497$ across $\rho{=}0.1$--$0.5$, a per-step inflation that
climbs from $1.5\times$ to $16.2\times$ and \emph{accelerates} with sparsity. We call this
\emph{super-exponential collapse}: the damage rate itself compounds. Two coupled mechanisms produce it.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Trust-region violation.} Each additional deletion is scored by a quadratic centered at
the \emph{dense} model, but evaluated at a far-pruned model; the approximation error compounds across
the budget rather than being re-zeroed.
\item \textbf{Operating-point drift.} Once the model is no longer the teacher, removing a unit changes
the \emph{already-accumulated} gap to $p_0$ to first order, a term that one-shot pruning cannot see
because it uses only the zero-drift dense state.
\end{itemize}
Because both arise from extrapolating a dense-state score, no one-shot criterion escapes the collapse.
This motivates rebuilding the selection surrogate along the budget.
